# The removal is a wall not a walk

Retire a fact that a dependant names in `uses`, that a link anchors through a scenario,
and that an open plan's task carries. The refusal reports all three in one message, as
required. Now clear them: the plan must be closed or repinned, the link retired with
`link rm --spec`, the dependant edited or removed — and editing the dependant may itself
refuse, because that fact has its own links and its own plan. Every step is a separate
verb and the order matters.

## Attractor

Deletion was the operation the wing was built around, and it becomes the most expensive
thing an operator can attempt. The rational response is not to delete: leave the false
fact standing, mark it in prose, work around it. The wing then accumulates exactly like
the spec it was built to give deletion pressure to, and the whole argument for its
existence quietly inverts.

## Resolution

The refusal becomes a runnable plan: derived `the-refusal-is-an-ordered-continuation`. The
blockers are already resolved by the pre-flight, so rendering them as ordered commands with
their arguments costs nothing and turns a wall into a walk. Nothing cascades —
`removal-names-the-code-it-strands` settled that a link is a recorded human assertion and no
verb retires one for the operator — but the operator no longer has to derive the sequence.
