# A verification repeats its requirement

Read the verifications in the plan just authored beside the requirements they answer for.
`- test — a Data-classified element is never reported, covered or not` appears verbatim in
both. The skill is explicit that a plan bullet must name the check in the frameworks the
user chose and must not paraphrase the requirement, and the author — who wrote that rule —
broke it on every task of the plan, and noticed only when asked a question about something
else.

## Attractor

The two layers collapse into one. The requirement's bullet says what must be proven and
the plan's bullet says how it will be proven here, and when they read the same the plan
has added nothing but a second place to edit. The implementer receives a restatement
instead of an instruction, and picks the test shape themselves — which is the drift the
plan exists to remove.

## Resolution

Accepted. A textual comparison catches verbatim repetition and misses the paraphrase, which
is both the common case and the worse one; a semantic comparison puts a judgement in a
checker. The two layers are held apart by where they are written and by the skill's own
instruction to name the test, and when they collapse the plan has added nothing rather than
broken something. Signed in `prose-is-not-checked-against-prose`.
