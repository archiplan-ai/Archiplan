# The rule against model nouns is prose

The rule that keeps a world fact from becoming a requirement in costume is: state it
without a noun from the model. It is written in the intent and in the reasoning behind
the origin split, and no requirement makes it checkable. `check` knows every element name
in the compiled graph, so it could say `this fact names Worktree`, and nothing asks it
to. The world's one content discipline is honour-based.

## Attractor

Facts drift into requirements one sentence at a time. `The operator learns late` becomes
`The registry must record who holds which checkout`, which resolves, parses and passes,
and now the same obligation lives on both sides with two different killers. The origin
rule — the thing that decides which side a claim belongs to — is enforced only by whoever
happens to review, and review is exactly what the world already spends its one human
budget on.

## Resolution

The rule becomes a finding: derived
`the-fact-speaks-the-world-and-check-says-when-it-does-not`. `check` already holds every
element name, so it names the fact and the element it matched, over the statement and the
conditioning paragraph only — the scenarios are exempt, because Gherkin describes the
system's surface and naming it there is the point. A finding and not an error: a fact may
legitimately quote a name, and whether it had to is the reader's judgement.
