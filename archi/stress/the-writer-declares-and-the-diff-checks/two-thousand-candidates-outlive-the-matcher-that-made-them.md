# two thousand candidates outlive the matcher that made them

Take the matcher's authority away and leave everything it already wrote in place.

The journal stands at 2036 captured candidates against 103 confirmed and 113 authored. They
are append-only truth: nothing removes them by aging, and the audit asks about every one
that decays below the floor — 229 rows do already, 48 of them on a single file written this
week. The proposal changes what capture does next. It says nothing about what capture has
already said.

The pile is not inert. It grades on every `verify`, it decays, it surfaces in the audit, and
each row still reads as a claim about what answers what. A reader who trusts the new
declarations has no way to tell them from two thousand guesses made by a rule that no longer
governs.

## Attractor

The pile is left alone because retiring it is a mass edit of append-only truth and nobody
wants to sign that. The audit's decay list grows past reading, so the audit stops being read,
and it took the confirmed and authored rows down with it. The reverse view is then computed
over a set that is mostly noise, and the first thing it says about any element is wrong.

## Resolution

Mark them, do not retire them: derived `the-journal-says-which-rule-made-a-row`. Every row
records what produced it — inferred, declared or authored — and the reverse view answers
from the last two alone. The 2036 inferred rows keep standing and keep grading, readable as
history and no longer read as evidence.

A mass edit of append-only truth was the obvious escape and is refused for the reason the
journal exists: a record that can be rewritten when it becomes inconvenient is not a record.
What the round does not solve is the audit's decay list, which is already past reading at
229 rows and will not shrink by being labelled. That is left standing and named here so the
next round can aim at it.
