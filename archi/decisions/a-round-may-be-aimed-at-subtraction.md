# A round may be aimed at subtraction

The stress ritual has no exit condition of its own. "Repeat until a round survives" reads
as convergence and behaves as growth: every fix adds surface, and the next round finds its
pressures on the surface the last one added. Six rounds on this intent ran three, three,
four, four, five and five breaks out of five — the rate rose monotonically.

We keep the ritual as it is. Bounding it by rule would mean a cap on rounds, a cap on
derived requirements per round, or a machine judgement about whether a design got harder to
break — and each of those refuses a genuine finding to satisfy a counter. The ritual's value
is that it finds real things, and it did on every round here.

What the seventh round did instead is the mitigation, and it is a practice rather than a
check: aim a round at the design's own size. Ask what the first author pays, what the tree
already holds, what was replaced that worked, and what six rounds took away. That round
retired a requirement, dropped a migration and reversed two decisions. Nothing in this
repository will remind anyone to run it.
